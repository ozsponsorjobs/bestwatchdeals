The History of the Captain Willard & Turtle Cushion Diver

In 1970, Seiko released Reference 6105-8110, an innovative 150-meter dive watch featuring an asymmetrical cushion-shaped steel case. The broad steel shoulders flared out around the 4 o'clock position to form an integrated crown guard, protecting the locking crown from underwater snags on coral and scuba equipment.

American soldiers deployed in Vietnam bought the 6105 at military PX stores in large numbers due to its rugged reliability in humid jungle environments. The watch was immortalized when Martin Sheen wore his personal 6105 while portraying Captain Benjamin Willard in Francis Ford Coppola's 1979 masterpiece Apocalypse Now.

While original vintage 6105 watches now sell for over $2,500 and require delicate handling, modern alternatives from Steeldive and Addiesdive feature modern Seiko NH35 automatic movements, 200m water resistance, ceramic bezels, and scratch-resistant sapphire crystals for under $130.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the crown positioned at 4 o'clock instead of 3 o'clock?
Positioning the crown at 4 o'clock is an ergonomic masterstroke: when you bend your wrist upward during swimming, typing, or physical work, the crown will never dig into the back of your hand. Furthermore, the asymmetric cushion case shields the crown from accidental blunt impacts.
Does a 44mm cushion case wear too large on small wrists?
Surprising to many, no. While the cushion case is 43.8mm wide, its lug-to-lug length is only 46.8mm (shorter than many 40mm dress watches). Because the lugs do not overhang the wrist, the SD1970 wears comfortably on wrists as small as 6.25 inches.
Are Steeldive and Addiesdive using real Seiko movements?
Yes. They use the Seiko Instruments Inc. (TMI) NH35A movement, which is the unbranded OEM version of the Seiko 4R35 movement manufactured by Seiko's subsidiary for independent watchmakers. It features 24 jewels, 21,600 bph, and Diashock shock protection.
